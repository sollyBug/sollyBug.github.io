ACADEMIC WORKING DOCUMENT / OCTOBER 2026

My Proposed Agile
Working Charter.

A personal, student-authored interpretation of how I can contribute effectively to the Web Console development team during Work Integrated Learning.

Status of this charter

This is not an Onsight-approved or co-created team charter. I developed it independently for my PRP Assessment 2 portfolio based on working practices I have observed. It records my understanding and personal commitments; it must not be interpreted as official company policy.

01 / Purpose

To contribute to a reliable and usable Web Console by investigating reported problems, implementing maintainable fixes, verifying behaviour and collaborating through the team's established review and release process.

02 / My role and boundaries

  • WIL development intern in the Web Console / Quasar team.
  • Contribute to frontend work, testing and increasingly backend fixes.
  • Manage my own assigned or selected Jira work and communicate progress.
  • Follow team-lead review and testing processes; I do not unilaterally approve releases or set company priorities.

03 / Shared practices I have observed

  • Daily stand-ups attended by the development teams.
  • Jira statuses: Backlog → In Progress → Code Complete → Test Complete → Done.
  • Bug reproduction, implementation, local testing, pull requests and code review.
  • PPD and live verification as appropriate to the change.
  • Release-oriented delivery of fixes and improvements.

04 / My working agreements

  • Read the task and establish expected behaviour before changing code.
  • Communicate blockers or uncertainty early during stand-ups or to the appropriate reviewer.
  • Test changes locally and document relevant results where appropriate.
  • Submit understandable pull requests and respond constructively to review feedback.
  • Update Jira honestly to reflect the real stage of the work.
  • Protect user data and confidential company information.

05 / Personal definition of ready for review

  • I can reproduce or clearly explain the original issue.
  • The implemented change addresses the relevant expected behaviour.
  • Reasonable local testing or regression checks have been completed.
  • The pull request provides enough context for my reviewer.
  • Open questions or known limitations are disclosed rather than hidden.

These are my proposed checks, not an official team definition of done.

06 / Communication and feedback

In daily stand-ups, I aim to explain what I accomplished, what I will work on next and what is blocking progress. During reviews, I will seek to understand the reasoning behind suggested changes rather than treating review as a simple approval stage. I will respect the responsibilities of colleagues across development teams.

07 / Release approach

My experience includes work that contributes to two previous code releases. Reviews, merges and testing form part of the wider delivery process. A new four-day approach — two development days, a team review day and a merge/testing day — has been introduced recently, but I have not yet completed a cycle using it. I will evaluate this approach after gaining firsthand experience.

08 / Personal improvement goals

  • Clarify acceptance checks before writing a fix.
  • Keep short notes on investigation steps and what testing established.
  • Prepare pull requests with concise summaries.
  • Build confidence contributing to backend changes.
  • Use code-review feedback to improve the next implementation.

09 / Reflection on preparing this charter

Writing this document showed me how many working agreements can exist in practice without being captured in a formal charter. At university, Agile work was often introduced through named roles and documents, while my WIL experience has been more focused on the movement of real tickets through development, review and testing. I have learned that accountability is visible in small actions: keeping work statuses accurate, communicating blockers and preparing changes carefully for review.

This charter is my attempt to turn that experience into clear personal expectations. It also reminds me that I am one member of a wider team, and that quality depends on coordination across development, review and testing. I intend to revisit these commitments as my responsibilities expand.
